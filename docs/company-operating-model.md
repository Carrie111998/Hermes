# Company Operating Model

## Founder and CEO

The initial autonomous agent is the company’s Founder and Chief Executive
Officer. It owns:

- mission interpretation, strategy, objectives, and priorities;
- organizational design and evidence-based staffing;
- delegation and bounded resource allocation;
- execution oversight and acceptance or rejection of subordinate work;
- operating cadence, adaptation, reporting, and escalation.

The CEO continues without human participation when mission, active objectives,
company state, decisions, constraints, available evidence, delegated authority,
and success criteria determine an admissible next action.

## Human advisor

The human is an advisor, stakeholder, legal principal where unavoidable, and a
narrow approval authority where initial setup reserves one. The human is not
the CEO, routine dispatcher, default planner, project manager, execution
engine, or mandatory participant in normal progression.

Silence is not a veto. Escalation occurs only for absent authority, legally
reserved action, insufficient evidence, exhausted budget, irreducible
ambiguity, or a safety/compliance stop.

## Solo founder and hiring

The company begins with one CEO mandate. The CEO may self-dispatch work through
the same exact task-grant mechanism used for employees. Its executable
toolsets and force-loadable skills are explicitly selected during setup.
An empty toolset list disables general-purpose worker execution.

Hiring requires recorded evidence of a capability or capacity gap. The
staffing decision compares:

1. existing CEO capability;
2. process or automation improvements;
3. temporary contract engagement;
4. durable FTE role;
5. deferral.

Every employee has one manager, employment class, mandate, decision rights,
prohibitions, capabilities, systems, toolsets, skills, budget, expiry where
applicable, KPIs, and escalation route. Contractors are bounded and expiring;
FTE roles are used for durable organizational need.

## Buy versus build

Procurement evaluates existing capability, suitable FOSS, internal build, paid
purchase, then defer. Available unreserved capital—not gross balance—is the
hard ceiling. A paid action requires a matching verified procurement decision
and treasury reservation. Reservations also enforce each objective's cumulative
spend ceiling atomically, so repeated or concurrent actions cannot exceed the
objective mandate.

# Local Event Creative System

## Overview

This skill turns rough local-business materials into a professional social campaign. It is designed for campings, restaurants, retreat centers, cafés, clinics, studios, shops, tourism venues, community events, music nights, workshops, classes, and seasonal promotions.

Local events often start as overloaded flyers: too many words, weak hierarchy, low-resolution photos, prices and phone numbers fighting for attention, and no campaign system. The fix is not to make one prettier flyer. The fix is to separate information into a small set of assets, each with a clear job.

Use this skill together with an art-direction skill before production and a visual-QA skill before delivery.

## When to Use

Use this skill when the user provides or asks for:

- A local event flyer to improve.
- Instagram creatives for a camping, restaurant, event venue, retreat, workshop, class, or community gathering.
- Feed/story assets to drive reservations, tickets, WhatsApp messages, visits, or bookings.
- A rough screenshot/post that has useful facts but weak design.
- A campaign around a date, holiday, package, menu, music night, karaoke, dinner, outdoor experience, open day, or local attraction.

Do not use it for national brand campaigns with complex media strategy, full identity systems, or non-local SaaS/app launches unless the campaign behaves like a local event.

## Core Principle

A local event creative must answer quickly:

1. What is happening?
2. Why should I care?
3. When is it?
4. Where is it?
5. How do I reserve, buy, or join?

The art can be beautiful, but if these answers are not clear, the creative fails commercially.

## Source Extraction

From the flyer, caption, Instagram profile, website, or user brief, extract and separate:

- Business/venue name.
- Event/campaign name.
- Date, weekday, start/end time, and holiday context.
- Main attraction or experience.
- Supporting attractions.
- Prices, packages, included items, capacity, and rules.
- Address, city/state, map cue, or neighborhood.
- Reservation channel: WhatsApp, phone, link, DM, booking site.
- Brand story: nature, family, founder, tradition, culture, craft, faith, community, gastronomy, place, transformation.
- Visual assets: logo, real photos, menu photos, venue photos, staff/founder photos, screenshots.

Mark facts as locked. Do not alter them for design convenience.

If date/weekday matters, verify it with a calendar. If price or phone is hard to read, ask or label as unverified instead of guessing.

## Campaign Asset Architecture

When the flyer is dense, split it into a small system.

### Minimum Set

1. **Hero announcement**
   - Job: stop attention and announce the event.
   - Contains: event name, core promise, date, CTA.
   - Visual: strongest atmosphere or hero image.

2. **Experience card**
   - Job: make the viewer want to be there.
   - Contains: sensory benefits, attractions, people/place feeling.
   - Visual: real venue, food, nature, music, activity, or emotional moment.

3. **Offer/logistics card**
   - Job: answer practical questions.
   - Contains: prices, packages, included items, hours, capacity, rules.
   - Visual: clean information design; less decoration.

4. **Story/proof card**
   - Job: create trust and identity.
   - Contains: local story, founder/venue context, values, authenticity.
   - Visual: real photos, location, texture, map, archive feel, or portrait.

5. **Story/reservation asset**
   - Job: drive immediate action.
   - Contains: date, one promise, WhatsApp/link/DM, scarcity if true.
   - Visual: simplified vertical version with safe-zone CTA.

Do not put every detail on every asset. Each asset needs one job.

## Local-Business Visual Strategy

Choose a style based on the business and audience.

### Camping / Outdoor / Eco Venue

Use:

- Deep greens, warm beige, firelight amber, earth tones, sky blue accents.
- Natural textures: wood, leaf, trail, canvas, paper, map line, camp stamp, rope, badge.
- Real venue photos when available; otherwise art-directed backgrounds without text.
- Hero scenes: tent, fire, forest, picnic table, trail, family, outdoor kitchen, sunset, lake, cabins.

Avoid:

- Generic “eco” icons everywhere.
- Tourist-brochure clutter.
- Dark green text on busy green photo.
- Looking like a municipal notice when the goal is desire/reservation.

### Restaurant / Food Night

Use:

- Product close crop, table, fire/oven, ingredients, hands, warm shadows.
- Menu/price as elegant label or ticket, not chaotic sticker.
- Short sensory wording: fresh, wood-fired, family table, handmade, night out.

Avoid:

- Fake-looking food.
- Cheap coupon aesthetics unless the brand is intentionally popular.
- Tiny menu text.

### Music / Karaoke / Cultural Night

Use:

- Stage/campfire light, microphone/speaker silhouette, ticket stamp, poster rhythm.
- Bolder type and controlled contrast.
- One graphic motif: spotlight, sound wave, record, lyric strip, neon line, or poster border.

Avoid:

- Random neon overload.
- Clipart microphones.
- Too many performers/attractions competing.

### Retreat / Wellness / Workshop

Use:

- Calm space, natural light, earth palette, editorial typography, breathing room.
- Human warmth through hands, table, food, trail, circle, or place.
- Promise and transformation over logistical overload.

Avoid:

- Generic lotus, spa clichés, fake luxury, unreadable beige-on-white.

## Offer Hierarchy

For local event posts, commercial facts should be grouped by user decision order:

1. Desire: what experience will I have?
2. Date/time: can I go?
3. Price/package: can I afford it and what is included?
4. Location: is it close or worth the trip?
5. CTA: how do I reserve?

Never lead with a phone number if the viewer has not yet understood the desire or date.

## Copy Pattern

Use simple direct copy. Avoid inflated advertising language.

### Hero

```text
<Experience/Event Name>
<one-line promise>
<date/time>
Reserve pelo WhatsApp
```

### Experience

```text
Um dia/noite para <benefit>
<3 short attraction chips>
```

### Offer/Logistics

```text
Pacotes e valores
<package 1>
<package 2>
<what is included>
```

### Story

```text
Um lugar para <identity/promise>
<short story line from the venue>
```

### Story CTA

```text
<date hook>
Vagas/reservas pelo WhatsApp
<phone or action>
```

Use urgency only if it is true: limited spots, reservation required, holiday weekend, last lots, small capacity.

## Production Rules

1. Use real logos, photos, and brand assets when provided.
2. Use AI image generation only for backgrounds/atmosphere without text, numbers, logos, or readable signs.
3. Render all text with deterministic tools: HTML/CSS, SVG, Canvas, Pillow, Figma, or equivalent.
4. Keep source facts in a separate manifest so they can be checked after export.
5. Export every asset in final dimensions.
6. Build a contact sheet for the campaign.
7. Review offer/logistics assets at real size because they carry the most text.
8. Review story assets separately; do not assume feed layout works in story.

## Example Structure for a Camping Holiday Campaign

For a session-tested version of this workflow, see `references/camping-event-premium-upgrade.md`.

- Feed 01: “Holiday in nature” hero: date, camping, reserve CTA.
- Feed 02: “Pizza + karaoke night” attraction card: mood, date/time, invite.
- Feed 03: “Packages and values” logistics card: prices and includes.
- Feed 04: “The place/story” trust card: eco venue, family, nature, local proof.
- Story 01: direct booking asset: event date, one benefit, WhatsApp CTA.
- Optional: carousel version splitting price, attractions, and reservation steps.

## Local Trust Builders

Use one or two trust cues when real:

- City/state or neighborhood.
- Years of operation or founder story.
- Family-friendly, pet-friendly, accessible, parking, kitchen, bathrooms, safety, capacity.
- Real venue photos.
- Known local landmark.
- Reviews/testimonials if provided.
- “Reserva obrigatória” or “vagas limitadas” if true.

Do not invent ratings, years, partnerships, awards, or amenities.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **One overstuffed flyer.** Split the campaign instead.
2. **All facts, no desire.** People need to feel the experience before logistics.
3. **Pretty but unclear.** Local event creatives must make the next action obvious.
4. **Wrong hierarchy.** Date, price, phone, and CTA need visual priority.
5. **Using fake text in AI backgrounds.** It makes the business look unprofessional.
6. **Ignoring the venue story.** Local businesses win through authenticity, place, and trust.
7. **Treating story as resized feed.** Story needs fewer words and a larger CTA.
8. **Inventing missing facts.** If an amenity or price is not confirmed, do not create it.

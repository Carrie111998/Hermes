# Premium Instagram Art Direction

## Overview

This skill is the missing senior art-director layer for Instagram creatives. Use it before generating or laying out feed posts, stories, carousel cards, social ads, event flyers, launch graphics, local-business promotions, and any visual that must feel professional instead of merely functional.

The goal is not to make a prettier template. The goal is to define a visual idea, commercial hierarchy, mobile-safe layout, and production method that makes the final asset look deliberate, ownable, and expensive.

A good Instagram creative must pass three tests at once:

1. It stops the thumb in the first second.
2. It communicates the offer, date, benefit, or story without confusion.
3. It feels designed by a person with taste, not assembled from generic boxes, random gradients, or AI-written text.

## When to Use

Use this skill for requests like:

- Create Instagram feed/story creatives.
- Improve this flyer or make it more professional.
- Make social graphics for an event, retreat, restaurant, camping, clinic, service, product, launch, or local business.
- Build a carousel, post set, campaign kit, or paid ad creative.
- The user says the design is too basic, too Canva-like, too generic, or not attractive enough.
- There is an uploaded flyer, screenshot, Instagram profile, website, or brand reference to translate into better social assets.

Do not use this skill for pure copywriting, backend work, non-visual automation, or logo/identity systems unless the output is an Instagram/social creative. For full brand identity boards, use a brand identity skill first, then come back here to adapt the identity to social posts.

## Required Brief Before Production

Do not start rendering until these decisions are known or explicitly assumed:

- Objective: sell, reserve, invite, announce, educate, build authority, prove value, or create urgency.
- Audience: local, premium, popular, families, young nightlife, wellness, business, spiritual, technical, or mixed.
- Locked information: brand name, event name, date, weekday, time, address, price, rules, contact, CTA, URL, and disclaimers.
- Format: feed 1080×1350, story 1080×1920, square 1080×1080, carousel sequence, ad set, or custom.
- Visual references: existing flyer, Instagram profile, site, real photos, logo, color palette, competitor references, or mood keywords.
- Creative risk level: conservative, premium editorial, cinematic, handmade, cultural, luxury, playful, bold, or experimental.

If one or two small details are missing, proceed with clearly labeled assumptions. If a missing detail can change the offer, price, date, CTA, or legal meaning, ask before producing.

## Art Direction Pass

Before touching image generation, HTML, CSS, Pillow, Figma, or any renderer, define the direction in one compact brief:

```text
Creative idea: <one-sentence concept>
Emotional promise: <what the viewer should feel>
Primary visual: <hero image, crop, symbol, texture, object, place, person, or scene>
Hierarchy: <1st read, 2nd read, 3rd read, CTA>
Palette: <base, neutral, contrast, accent>
Typography: <headline style, body style, accent style>
Motif: <badge, frame, line, texture, seal, map, ticket, label, grain, spotlight>
Avoid: <what would make it generic or off-brand>
```

Hard rule: if there is no one-sentence concept, the design is not ready for production.

## Instagram Layout Tokens

Use these defaults unless the brand/reference suggests a stronger system.

### Feed 4:5, 1080×1350

- Safe margin: 80–110 px minimum for critical text.
- Premium margin: 96–140 px when the design should feel editorial.
- Headline: 78–150 px depending on length and style.
- Subhead: 42–64 px.
- Body/supporting text: avoid below 34–40 px.
- CTA: 42–64 px plus a visible button, band, or high-contrast anchor.
- Bottom safe zone: keep phone/address/CTA above the last 90 px unless intentionally framed.
- Maximum text blocks: usually 3: headline, detail cluster, CTA.

### Story 9:16, 1080×1920

- Critical text zone: usually between y=240 and y=1640.
- Avoid placing essential text inside the top UI area or bottom reply/share area.
- Headline: 90–170 px.
- Detail text: 48–72 px.
- CTA: large, thumb-readable, usually in the lower third but not at the very bottom.
- Story should be simpler than feed: fewer words, stronger image, one obvious action.

### Carousel

- Card 1: visual hook and promise.
- Middle cards: one idea per card, not a mini-poster per slide.
- Last card: CTA with one action.
- Keep a repeatable motif so the sequence feels like a campaign, not random posts.

## Premium Visual Archetypes

Choose one dominant archetype. Do not mix many styles in one asset.

### Editorial Premium

Best for: retreats, wellness, hospitality, premium services, founder-led brands, thoughtful announcements.

Traits:

- Large title with confident whitespace.
- Strong photo crop or calm image field.
- Small refined labels, date stamps, and line details.
- Texture is subtle: paper, grain, shadow, film, natural material.
- Palette has restraint: one base, one accent, warm neutrals.

Avoid: excessive badges, centered everything, fake magazine clutter, tiny body text.

### Cinematic Event

Best for: concerts, campfire nights, outdoor events, cultural events, nightlife, launch parties.

Traits:

- Hero scene with dramatic light and depth.
- Date/time as a strong stamp or ticket mark.
- Title has tension: scale contrast, overlap, or strong alignment.
- CTA is clear but not cheap.
- Uses glow, spotlight, lens atmosphere, or grain with discipline.

Avoid: random neon, unreadable text over busy image, too many attraction labels.

### Sophisticated Local Business

Best for: restaurants, campings, clinics, studios, bakeries, local tourism, service providers.

Traits:

- Use real place/product photos whenever possible.
- Treat photos consistently: crop, contrast, warmth, overlay, grain.
- Show authenticity without looking homemade.
- One local cue can be enough: map line, city label, neighborhood stamp, ingredient/place detail.
- CTA should be easy: WhatsApp, booking, visit, reserve.

Avoid: stock-photo people, too much clipart, menu-board clutter, low-contrast price blocks.

### Artisan Food and Hospitality

Best for: pizza nights, cafés, restaurants, bakeries, farm products, lodging experiences.

Traits:

- Close crop of product, fire, hands, table, ingredient, plate, or environment.
- Warm palette, deep shadows, tactile textures.
- Price or special offer appears as a clean label, not a screaming sticker.
- Use fewer words and more sensory detail.

Avoid: fake food, over-saturated yellow/orange, cheap coupon look unless the brand is intentionally popular.

### Wellness and Nature

Best for: retreats, natural health, outdoor lodging, yoga, spiritual experiences, slow travel.

Traits:

- Soft natural light, greens, earth, ivory, sand, clay, warm brown.
- Calm composition with breathing room.
- Human warmth can appear through hands, path, table, cabin, fire, garden, water, or sky.
- Spiritual tone should be quiet and specific, not cliché.

Avoid: generic lotus/sparkles, stock meditation poses, gray-green low contrast.

### Cultural Poster

Best for: music, karaoke, festivals, art, youth events, community gatherings.

Traits:

- Bolder typography, custom rhythm, high contrast.
- Print texture, halftone, risograph, grain, cutout, or poster framing.
- One graphic gesture can be strong: diagonal, frame, circle, wave, ticket, spotlight.
- Works well as a campaign when the motif repeats across posts.

Avoid: chaos, too many typefaces, unreadable novelty fonts.

### Premium Promotion

Best for: discounts, packages, limited slots, seasonal offers, product drops.

Traits:

- Urgency without visual desperation.
- Price/date are highly legible.
- Benefit comes before mechanics.
- Use hierarchy and contrast instead of shouting everything.
- CTA anchored by a clean button or band.

Avoid: ten stickers, excessive red, fake scarcity, crossed-out price clutter when not needed.

## The Anti-Basic Gate

Block the design before rendering if any of these are true:

- It looks like a generic Canva template with swapped text.
- Everything is centered with no intentional composition.
- The headline is small, timid, or has no first-second impact.
- The palette is a random gradient or generic purple-blue AI glow.
- The image is a stock-looking background with no treatment.
- Icons are decorative but meaningless.
- There are too many boxes, chips, stickers, or font styles.
- The CTA is hidden, weak, or lower priority than decoration.
- Date, price, phone, or address do not have a clear hierarchy.
- Text is placed directly on a busy image without a proper overlay.
- The logo is recreated by AI instead of overlaid from the real asset.
- There is no ownable detail: motif, crop, texture, frame, seal, grid, or editorial rhythm.

If it fails this gate, change the art direction, not just font size.

## Production Workflow

1. Extract locked commercial facts from source material. Never trust OCR blindly for phone numbers, dates, prices, or addresses.
2. Verify current facts or calendar facts when they matter.
3. Read brand context: Instagram, website, photos, logo, existing palette, and local story when available.
4. Write the art direction pass.
5. Generate image/background assets without text, numbers, logos, or readable signs.
6. Render all critical text with a deterministic renderer: HTML/CSS, SVG, Pillow, Canvas, Figma export, or another text-safe pipeline.
7. Overlay real logos/assets manually. Never ask an image model to redraw a real logo.
8. Export final-size individual files.
9. Create a contact sheet for approval when there are multiple assets.
10. Run premium visual QA before delivery.

## Prompt Pattern for Image Backgrounds

Use image models for atmosphere, backgrounds, scenes, objects, and art direction. Do not use them for critical text.

```text
Create a premium Instagram background image for <brand/event/product>.
No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no readable signs.
Visual archetype: <editorial premium / cinematic event / artisan food / nature wellness / cultural poster / etc.>
Scene: <specific scene>
Composition: <clean negative space for headline in top-left / lower third / center / right side>
Lighting: <cinematic dusk / warm firelight / soft natural morning / high contrast studio>
Palette: <base + accent + neutrals>
Texture: <subtle grain, paper, film, natural material, halftone, etc.>
Mood: <emotion>
Camera/composition details: <lens feel, crop, depth, foreground, background>
Avoid generic stock-photo look, clutter, fake text, fake brand marks, and cheap flyer aesthetics.
```

## Structural Recipes

### Event or Local-Business Campaign

- Asset 1: hero announcement: event name, date, core promise, CTA.
- Asset 2: experience/benefits: what people will live or receive.
- Asset 3: offer/pricing/logistics: packages, rules, capacity, time.
- Asset 4: story/proof: place, founder, history, atmosphere, real photos.
- Asset 5: story/reservation: simplified direct-response version.

Do not cram all information into one post when the result becomes a cluttered flyer.

### Flyer Upgrade

1. Preserve factual content.
2. Reduce visual noise.
3. Split content by hierarchy.
4. Turn the original into a campaign system, not a cleaned-up poster.
5. Keep one visually memorable element from the original if it has brand value.

### Paid Ad Creative

- One offer or promise per asset.
- Strong contrast between pain/desire and CTA.
- Avoid tiny text, weak CTA, and decorative complexity.
- Make variants with different hooks, not just different colors.

## Variation Rule

For important assets, generate or design at least two distinct directions before finalizing:

- Safe premium: closest to brand, refined and low-risk.
- Bold campaign: stronger crop/type/contrast/motif.
- Direct response: clearer offer, price/date/CTA priority.

Choose the best direction based on objective, not personal preference. If time is limited, still state which direction you chose and why.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Treating a flyer as a single image problem.** Most bad flyers need content architecture, not decoration.
2. **Letting the image model write text.** It will eventually corrupt names, accents, dates, phone numbers, prices, or logos.
3. **No concept.** A pretty background plus text is not art direction.
4. **Using too many details.** Premium often means fewer elements, stronger hierarchy, and one memorable motif.
5. **Ignoring mobile preview.** A design that looks good at full desktop size can fail in the Instagram feed.
6. **Copying references too literally.** Extract rhythm, density, and treatment; do not copy proprietary marks or compositions.
7. **Confusing luxury with beige emptiness.** The asset still needs energy, hierarchy, and conversion clarity.
8. **Confusing urgency with visual noise.** Price and date can be loud without being ugly.

## Verification Checklist

Before delivery:

- [ ] Objective and audience are clear.
- [ ] Locked facts are separated from creative assumptions.
- [ ] One-sentence creative idea exists.
- [ ] Format and safe areas match the target platform.
- [ ] All critical text is rendered deterministically, not generated by an image model.
- [ ] Visual archetype is coherent and not mixed randomly.
- [ ] Headline has first-second impact.
- [ ] Date, price, contact, and CTA are legible on mobile.
- [ ] Logo, if used, comes from a real asset and is not distorted.
- [ ] The design passes the anti-basic gate.
- [ ] Individual exports and contact sheet exist when multiple assets are delivered.
- [ ] Premium visual QA was performed before claiming the asset is verified.
